# ADR 020: Project-owned character, skeleton, and motion representation

Status: Accepted for implementation

## Decision

Videoer owns the character definition and a canonical humanoid skeleton with metres, right-handed coordinates, Y-up, and a documented forward axis. Characters reference versioned topology, continuous body/face parameters, skeleton, skin weights, morphs, materials, hair, clothing, accessories, identity references, and verification artifacts.

Rendered experiments reject the original overlapping-capsule mesh as a production body: the current heroine has five disconnected skin components, 92 boundary edges, zero blended vertices across all 19 major joint zones, and no hand/foot/toe-owned body vertices. A project-authored implicit surface now supplies one closed manifold body, explicit extremity volumes, deterministic anatomical-chain weighting, and renderer-independent preserve-volume deformation. It is the accepted structural foundation, not a visually accepted final character. Its first dual-angle walk review still rejects proportions, joint contours, hands, feet, face, hair, silhouette, motion naturalism, and skin response. Fine anatomical topology and identity work may therefore introduce a legally clean project-owned template or automated retopology on top of this foundation; the experiment does not justify polishing the implicit mannequin indefinitely. No commercially restrictive parametric human system may become foundational.

The selected stable-topology experiment is the pinned CC0 MakeHuman hm08 base and its CC0 authored weights, converted into Videoer's existing domain representation without invoking or shipping MakeHuman application code. The canonical joint IDs and hierarchy remain stable. The production rest pose is anatomical A-pose; proxy-authored motion is retargeted through an explicit upper-arm/forearm profile rather than rotating clavicles or injecting descendant wrist corrections. Reducing the source's separate toe chains to one locomotion toe joint uses the metatarsal `foot-1` pivot and a deterministic shared-hinge weight transition. This is a demonstrated structural correction: the earlier toe-tip pivot and naive weight collapse failed rendered and surface-strain verification.

The reduction is not the final production deformation rig and portability is not a reason to render a hero character through lower-quality deformation. A 2026-08-31 audit proved that MPFB 2.0.17 at commit `437dd513888a92399d1d3200d2e80859fae55abc` uses the exact byte-identical hm08 OBJ already pinned by Videoer, supplies a 163-bone authored default deform rig with 28 individual toe bones, and can generate a 1,090-bone Rigify human-with-toes control rig with 209 deform bones. Videoer's older MakeHuman weight file contains 139 groups and the current four-influence/canonical collapse discards twist, corrective, and individual-toe structure. That is a plausible cause of visible hand, wrist, foot, and toe defects and must be compared rather than defended as architecture. Production shots use the highest-quality verified rig/deformation representation justified by their quality tier; the canonical reduction exists for authoring, interchange, compatibility, proxy evaluation, and retargeting, then drives the full production rig.

Videoer's canonical 52-joint skeleton remains the renderer-independent motion, interaction, retargeting, and compatibility contract. MPFB/Rigify is an open-source Blender production-control/deformation backend behind an explicit canonical mapping; it does not replace the domain skeleton and campaign definitions must never store Rigify bone names. Motion is authored or synthesized once over canonical joints, adapted to the generated control rig, evaluated against the full authored weights, grounded against the final evaluated surface, and baked for deterministic rendering. The adapter history is retained as evidence: v1 copied control translations and stretched the fingers; v3 grounded only the lowest evaluated sole point and therefore concealed a 47–50 mm terminal-stance forefoot failure; v7 introduced the profile-owned coordinate conversion, Rigify IK/FK snapping, non-stretch leg IK, and coupled heel/forefoot contact solve. V8 then measured the apparent leg crossing and disproved it: the inherited production A-pose placed evaluated soles about 400 mm apart. A first 94 mm canonical walking-base target was also rejected when v9 found only 45 mm evaluated separation and an 11 mm centreline crossing. V19 rejected support-root overshoot at 1.32–1.38 of the actual support-sole offset. V22 preserves the recalibrated solve at 1.17–1.22, 86.1 mm minimum separation, 14.4 mm side margin, four-finger transfer within 2.39 degrees, and thumb-to-index opposition within 0.046 normalized distance-ratio error. Phase-gait v3 replaces exact pelvis/thorax/shoulder/arm synchronization with distinct C2-continuous waveforms; accumulated global thorax yaw has a measured 0.0625-cycle opposition delay. Five-view evidence remains visually rejected for mannequin-like arm carriage, narrow frontal tracking/apparent hip sway, and weak loading/release. MPFB therefore solves access to mature deformation/control machinery; it does not make an immature motion clip natural automatically.

Motion is stored as engine-owned clips and procedural actions over canonical bone IDs. It supports interpolation, additive blending, root motion, joint constraints, IK targets, gaze, and retarget metadata. Interactions are phase-based domain records resolving actors, props, and named scene attachment points at runtime. `open-door` and `read-book` are required objective fixtures. [ADR 024](024-research-grounded-phase-motion-synthesis.md) fixes the generic phase, contact, pose-layer, and acceptance architecture and applies it first to research-grounded human gait.

## Consequences

Three.js and Blender rigs are adapters. Production-human geometry declares dual-quaternion skinning in the domain contract; the CPU evaluator implements it directly and Blender maps it to armature preserve-volume mode. Other adapters must implement that declared mode before claiming compatibility. A character is accepted only after static pose, raised-arm, bent-elbow, bent-knee, walk, turn, reach, face, hand, hair, material, and deformation probes pass mechanical and hash-bound visual inspection. A render completing successfully or a foot touching the ground is not motion acceptance.

Every renderer-boundary rig profile owns its coordinate conversion. The MPFB hm08 body faces Blender `-Y`, so `mpfb-rigify-human-toes-v1` maps canonical left/up/forward to Blender `+X/+Z/-Y`. It must not reuse the generic Blender geometry conversion. Travel direction and sampled elbow-angle equivalence are fail-closed adapter gates so a coordinate mismatch cannot again produce a body that faces forward while travelling backward or convert a mild arm swing into a guarded pose.

Grounded production motion is verified against the final dual-quaternion-deformed surface. The periodic correction curve is bandwidth-limited and conservatively offset before keyframing so changing lowest-contact vertices cannot manufacture root jitter. Proxy contact reconstruction remains valid for proxy-authored clips, but it must not override final-mesh grounding for a production character. Walking deformation reports cover shoulders, hands, feet, and toes at repeated gait phases and reject unexpected base-walk hand tracks, excess edge strain, collapsed area, or triangle inversion.

The same rule applies to the MPFB/Rigify backend: grounding and deformation evidence must be measured from its evaluated output, not copied from the reduced canonical skin. A global lowest-point correction is insufficient: the verifier identifies anatomical heel and forefoot regions on the evaluated body and checks them independently at initial contact, midstance, terminal stance, and midswing. Lateral verification also measures evaluated sole centres, minimum/maximum walking-base width, anatomical side order, support under/overshoot, and root position relative to the actual support sole. Hand transfer separately compares four-finger flexion deltas and thumb-to-index opposition distance against each rig's own rest hand; a thumb is not graded as another finger hinge. Canonical arm/clavicle amplitude uses quaternion geodesic orientation sweep because local Euler-channel range changes under rest-pose retargeting. Canonical thorax verification accumulates hips + spine + chest and measures the strongest cyclic opposition lag; local chest opposition alone cannot hide a globally following or mechanically synchronized trunk. The experimental adapter and evidence live in `scripts/blender/render_mpfb_motion_probe.py` and `campaigns/reference-cinematic-benchmark/work/rig-audit/`; the current evidence is `mpfb-motion-comparison-v22/adapter.json` plus its side, front-three-quarter, frontal, hand, and foot renders/contact sheets. The mechanical adapter gates pass, but the backend remains experimental until whole-body dynamics, smoothness, silhouette separation, and multi-angle visual gates pass on the same canonical clip.
